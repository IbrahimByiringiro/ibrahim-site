**How Rwanda's $10 million Statistics Training Centre and Data Science Campus aimed to strengthen statistical capacity, embrace emerging technologies, and support evidence-based decision-making.**

In May 2019, I joined CNBC Africa to discuss Rwanda's newly unveiled $10 million Statistics Training Centre and Data Science Campus. At the time, I was serving as Director of Statistical Methods, Research and Publication at the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda (NISR).

The conversation focused on a question that remains relevant today: **How can countries turn the growing volume of available data into meaningful insights that drive development?**

## Why Rwanda Needed to Invest in Data Science

During the interview, I emphasized that the investment came at a critical time, when demand for timely, reliable data was growing rapidly.

Governments and businesses increasingly needed accurate information to make decisions, while advances in technology were generating unprecedented amounts of data.

Rwanda had already begun digitalizing government services and had adopted its National Data Revolution Policy in April 2017. However, translating that ambition into practice required more than policy commitments.

It required modern infrastructure, advanced IT systems, skilled professionals, and strong partnerships between government, academia, and the private sector.

The new Statistics Training Centre and Data Science Campus represented an important investment in building that capacity.

## Moving Beyond Traditional Statistics

One of the key points I highlighted was Rwanda's progress in improving the frequency and availability of official statistics.

Traditional statistical programs, including household surveys, censuses, and economic reporting, remained essential. Rwanda had also increased the frequency of important statistical outputs, including quarterly labor force and GDP statistics.

But traditional methods alone could not fully capture the opportunities presented by emerging technologies.

Mobile phones, social networks, internet platforms, and satellites were producing enormous amounts of data that could potentially complement conventional statistical sources.

Harnessing these alternative sources could help generate more timely insights, improve public services, support business development, and strengthen policymaking.

## From Data Collection to Data-Driven Decisions

Collecting more data is not necessarily the same as making better decisions.

The real value comes from transforming raw information into reliable statistics, meaningful insights, and practical solutions.

This requires investment in people as much as technology. Statistical training, research capacity, modern analytical tools, and collaboration across institutions are essential to building a sustainable data ecosystem.

As I explained during the interview, the new facility was not simply an investment in physical infrastructure. It was intended to support Rwanda's immediate statistical needs while preparing the country for future technological developments.

## Looking Ahead: The Future of Data in Development

Our discussion also touched on the upcoming International Conference on Big Data in Kigali, highlighting Rwanda's growing interest in exploring innovative approaches to official statistics.

Looking back, the central message of that conversation remains relevant: **The future of statistics lies in combining strong statistical foundations with emerging data sources and technologies.**

For countries pursuing digital transformation, the challenge is not merely generating more data, but developing the capacity to use it responsibly, efficiently, and effectively.

Rwanda's investment in the Statistics Training Centre and Data Science Campus was an important step toward that ambition.

And it reflects a principle that continues to guide my work in statistics and data science: **Data creates value when it informs decisions and improves lives.**
