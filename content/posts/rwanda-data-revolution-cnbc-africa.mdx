In May 2019, I joined CNBC Africa to discuss Rwanda's newly unveiled $10 million Statistics Training Centre and Data Science Campus. At the time, I was serving as Director of Statistical Methods, Research and Publication at the National Institute of Statistics of Rwanda (NISR).

Our conversation focused on a simple question: How can Rwanda make better use of data to support its development?

Rwanda had already made significant progress in digitalizing public services and improving the availability of official statistics. But with growing demand for timely information and new data sources emerging from mobile phones, satellites, and digital platforms, traditional statistical methods alone were no longer enough.

The new centre aimed to bridge that gap by investing in modern technology, training statistical professionals, and strengthening collaboration between government, researchers, and the private sector.

One point I emphasized during the interview was that collecting more data doesn't automatically lead to better decisions. What matters is having the skills, tools, and systems to turn that data into useful information.

Looking back, the message remains just as relevant today. As artificial intelligence and other technologies continue to evolve, countries need both strong statistical foundations and the capacity to embrace innovation.

This is also a principle that continues to guide my work: Data creates real value when it helps people make better decisions and improve lives.
